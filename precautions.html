Precautions & Prevention

Most snakebites are preventable. These are low-cost, practical habits that meaningfully reduce risk.

At home

🏠 Clear the surroundings
Keep grass short and brush cleared near walls. Snakes hide in tall grass and debris piles.
🐀 Control rodents
Rodents attract snakes that hunt them. Store grain and food securely, and address rodent problems early.
🚪 Seal entry points
Close gaps under doors and cracks in walls or foundations where snakes could enter.
🛏️ Sleep raised, use a net
Sleep on a raised bed rather than the floor where possible, and use a tucked-in mosquito net.
🪵 Store firewood away
Keep firewood and stone piles away from the house — they're common snake hiding spots.
👞 Check shoes and bedding
Shake out shoes and check bedding before use in snake-prone regions, especially after rain.

Outdoors — walking, farming, hiking

👢 Wear closed shoes or boots
Not sandals. This alone significantly reduces bite risk while walking through grass or fields.
🔦 Carry a light at night
Most bites happen when a snake is startled or stepped on in the dark. Always carry a flashlight.
🥾 Use a stick to probe ahead
In tall grass or during farm work, tap the ground ahead of you rather than stepping blindly.
🤚 Don't reach blindly
Avoid putting hands or feet into holes, under rocks, logs, or dense vegetation you can't see into.
🌧️ Extra care in monsoon
Snakebite risk rises June–September as flooding pushes snakes into fields and homes.
🐍 Give snakes space
Snakes bite defensively, not to attack. If you see one, back away slowly rather than approaching or trying to move it.

If you're bitten anyway

Prevention reduces risk but can't eliminate it. If a bite happens: stay calm, immobilize the limb below heart level, remove tight clothing/jewelry near the bite, and get to a hospital immediately. Don't cut, suck, ice, or tourniquet the wound. See Help & Support for the full first-aid guide, or use the chat assistant on the home page.
